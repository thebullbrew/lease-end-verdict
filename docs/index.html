The method

Turnover is seasonal, rent is not. Landlords negotiate rent to the dollar and then sign any end date the tenant suggests. That is backwards. A vacant unit in January burns lost rent, utilities, and showing time in the worst leasing month of the year — while the identical unit in June often has a waitlist.

The fix is term, not hope. You usually cannot move a tenant's move-in date. You can almost always move the term: offer 13, 15, or 18 months on the first lease so every renewal after it lands in the prime window. Tenants rarely care. Your vacancy line cares a lot.

Student landlords already know this. An August-to-August lease is prime by design. This tool gives every other landlord the same discipline — and a dollar figure to bring to the negotiation when a tenant asks for the term that lands you in the trap.

Season multipliers are plain-spoken planning defaults (Jan 2.10× down to Jun–Jul 0.70×), scaled by your market-heat pick. Set your peak-season baseline from your own last three fills for the sharpest read. Not legal advice; lease terms must comply with local law.
